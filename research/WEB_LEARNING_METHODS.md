## Evidence-to-feature decisions

| Method | Primary evidence checked | Product decision and limit |
|---|---|---|
| Retrieval | [Karpicke & Roediger 2007](https://learninglab.psych.purdue.edu/downloads/2007/2007_Karpicke_Roediger_JML.pdf), abstract and Experiments1–2 | Require an attempted answer before reveal; retain successful cards in later reviews. The study did not test CLAT preparation. |
| Spacing | [Cepeda et al. 2008](https://www.evullab.org/pdf/CepedaVulRohrerWixtedPashler-PS-2008.pdf), p. 1095 and methods/results | Review across days with intervals sensitive to the deadline. A 1/3/7-day sequence is a simple implementation heuristic, not a validated legal-learning optimum. |
| Feedback | [Butler, Karpicke & Roediger 2008](https://learninglab.psych.purdue.edu/downloads/2008/2008_Butler_Karpicke_Roediger_JEPLMC.pdf), p. 918 and Experiments1–2 | Explain all answers, including uncertain correct ones; offer a repair queue. Do not restrict learning feedback to wrong responses or claim one feedback timing is universally optimal. |
| Interleaving | [Rohrer & Taylor 2007](https://digitalcommons.usf.edu/psy_facpub/1767/), author-university record and abstract, Experiment 2 | After initial topic learning, mix previously studied problems so the student must identify which rule fits. Evidence from mathematics is not direct proof of benefit in law. |
| Self-explanation | [Chi et al. 1989](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1207/s15516709cog1302_1), publisher abstract | Prompt issue, rule, decisive fact, exception and conclusion before the worked answer. The small observational mechanics study does not establish a causal score gain from this exact legal template. |
| Calibration | [Koriat & Bjork 2005](https://cris.haifa.ac.il/en/publications/illusions-of-competence-in-monitoring-ones-knowledge-during-study/), author-university record/abstract | Collect confidence before revealing the answer; separate familiarity from later retrieval. Confidence is not an automatically calibrated success probability. |

## Practical 19-day implementation

An example 180-minute day allocates 30 minutes to due recall,45 to a focused lesson,45 to timed passages,30 to mixed questions,20 to error repair and10 to planning. This allocation is editorial. When time is limited, reduce the inflow of new material before removing corrective feedback. On the final 72 hours, prioritise reviewable mistakes and short mixed practice rather than unbounded new content. Neither the exact allocation nor taper has been tested as an optimal CLAT intervention.

Avoid claims that more difficult feelings always mean more learning, that card completion equals mastery, that rereading never helps, that interleaving should start before basic understanding, or that an algorithm can guarantee easy marks. A plausible opportunity is to reduce personally observed errors and strengthen distinctions already in reach; verify progress on later unseen attempts.
